Optional interactive activities
These activities describe future tap-to-answer interactions. In this PDF, read the options, choose mentally, then compare with the feedback. No personal substance-use history is requested. Any question may be skipped.

Activity 1 — Identify the kind of risk Question: Someone who drank alcohol has trouble coordinating movements and plans to ride home. Is the main immediate concern (A) an impaired-driving injury risk, (B) guaranteed future dependence, or (C) no risk if the person feels awake? Feedback: A is best. Alcohol can impair movement and judgment immediately. This alone cannot predict who will develop dependence. [1,3]

Activity 2 — Long-term health Question: Which statement is accurate? (A) Alcohol causes cancer only after addiction, (B) Alcohol increases risk of certain cancers even without an alcohol use disorder, or (C) only spirits carry cancer risk. Feedback: B is accurate. The carcinogenic exposure is ethanol in alcoholic beverages. Risk is not restricted to people diagnosed with alcohol use disorder. [1,5]

More optional interactive learning
Activity 3 — Medicines and combinations Fictional choice: A friend offers an unprescribed sedative after someone has been drinking. Choose: (A) Accept because it is medication; (B) Refuse, avoid mixing, and obtain medical advice if needed. Feedback: B is safer. Alcohol with sedating substances may dangerously increase impairment and breathing suppression. [3,6]

Activity 4 — Possible overdose Fictional choice: Someone is unconscious with slow breathing. Choose: (A) Let them sleep alone; (B) Seek emergency medical help immediately, stay with them, and follow responder instructions. Feedback: B is safer. Do not assume someone with abnormal breathing is merely asleep. [6]

Activity 5 — Compassionate support Fictional choice: A student has become withdrawn and is missing lessons. Choose: (A) Publicly accuse them of addiction; (B) Speak privately, avoid assumptions, and seek appropriate support if there are concerns. Feedback: B is more appropriate. Changes in behavior may have many causes and the young person deserves privacy and safeguarding. [4]
