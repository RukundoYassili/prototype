Optional interactive learning - question and feedback scripts
These examples will become interactive choices on the website. The PDF presents the question, options, recommended response, and explanation. They are not a diagnostic tool and collect no personal history. A user may skip or retry.

Activity 1 - Identify the term Question: A patient has withdrawal symptoms after suddenly stopping a correctly prescribed medicine. What can we conclude? A. This always proves addiction. B. Physical dependence is possible, but addiction cannot be assumed. C. A person should always stop treatment immediately. Recommended answer: B. Physical adaptation and addiction are distinct. Medication decisions require clinical guidance. [3,4]

Activity 2 - Recognize risk Question: Someone drives after drinking but has no history of dependence. Which statement is accurate? A. There is no health risk without addiction. B. Impairment and injury can occur without addiction. C. A quiz can verify driving ability. Recommended answer: B. A single episode of use can create danger. Do not drive or ride while impaired.

Activity 3 - Respond without stigma Question: A friend says they cannot reduce substance use despite repeated harm. What is a helpful response? A. Shame them publicly. B. Encourage confidential professional assessment and listen. C. Diagnose them from the story. Recommended answer: B. Support focuses on respect, specific concerns, and appropriate care.

Activity 4 - Youth pressure Question: A student refuses alcohol but friends continue insisting. What might help? A. Accept to prove maturity. B. State a boundary and seek a safe supporter. C. Take unknown medication instead. Recommended answer: B. Personal boundaries and supportive adults can reduce pressure; the young person should not be required to disclose details publicly.

Activity 5 - Withdrawal safety Question: A person with a history of prolonged heavy drinking wants to quit immediately and is worried about shaking. What is the safest advice? A. Attempt unsupervised detoxification. B. Seek prompt medical advice about withdrawal. C. Replace alcohol with sedative pills from a friend. Recommended answer: B. Withdrawal can require medical assessment and treatment; do not give self-directed detox regimens. [5]
