Understanding Consent, Boundaries, and Bodily Autonomy
Optional learning activities

Keep any reflections private. Nothing is submitted. You may skip any activity. These are educational drafts; original qualifications and safety notes apply. Avoid saving personal details on a device someone else can access.

Activity 1 — Consent or pressure? (10 minutes) Read each fictional sentence and decide whether it shows freely given agreement: (a) “Yes, I want to participate,” after an open, pressure-free choice; (b) “Fine, I agree,” after threats to remove financial support; (c) no reply while someone moves closer; (d) permission to share one photograph privately. Suggested answers: (a) may indicate consent if informed, specific and the person has capacity; (b) coercion undermines voluntary choice; (c) silence is not consent; (d) permission does not extend to public posting.

Activity 2 — Everyday boundaries (10 minutes) Write three harmless fictional situations involving shared space, photographs, and time. For each, draft one respectful request and one respectful refusal. Example: “May I upload this photo?” / “No, please keep it private.” No personal history is required.

Activity 3 — Power mapping (15 minutes) Choose a fictional school or workplace situation. Identify who controls grades, pay, references, accommodation, or reputation. Discuss what an independent reporting channel would look like and how anonymity or confidentiality limits should be explained.

Activity 4 — Digital consent decision tree (10 minutes) Imagine being asked to forward a friend’s personal message. Ask: Was permission given? For this audience and purpose? Could sharing create harm? If permission is absent, do not share. If safety is already threatened, seek appropriate support rather than negotiating with a threatening person.

Activity 5 — Supportive response practice (15 minutes) Using a purely fictional disclosure, practice a brief response that listens, validates, checks immediate safety, and asks what assistance is wanted. Do not role-play graphic violence or require participants to disclose personal experiences. Anyone may opt out.
