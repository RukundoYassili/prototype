Domestic Violence and Intimate Partner Violence
Optional learning activities

Keep any reflections private. Nothing is submitted. You may skip any activity. These are educational drafts; original qualifications and safety notes apply. Avoid saving personal details on a device someone else can access.

Activity 1 - Identify the pattern Read Scenario B. List three behaviors that could indicate coercive control. Then ask: would the student be free to refuse without fear? Sample answer: demands for private messages, constant location surveillance, and threats of humiliation; fear of retaliation suggests the requests are not freely negotiated boundaries.

Activity 2 - Practice a supportive response Imagine someone says: “My partner threatens me whenever I try to visit my friends.” Write a two-sentence response that validates their experience without instructing them what to do. Sample: “That sounds frightening, and you deserve to be treated with respect. Would you like to talk about what kind of support would feel safe for you?”

Activity 3 - Separate risk factors from blame Consider a household with unemployment and harmful alcohol use. Explain why these factors may be relevant to prevention but do not excuse violence. Sample answer: context can influence risk, but responsibility rests with the person using violence.

Activity 4 - Privacy and digital safety Identify why texting detailed safety instructions to a monitored phone could be dangerous. Sample answer: the abusive person may see the messages, increase surveillance, or retaliate. Safer communication options require careful discussion with a qualified supporter.

Activity 5 - Referral mapping Identify two official services from the Rwanda contact table and explain their published roles. Do not make test calls to crisis lines as an exercise. A community organization should verify contacts through official channels before publishing locally tailored materials.
