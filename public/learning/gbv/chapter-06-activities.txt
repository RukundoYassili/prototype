Sexual Violence, Harassment, and Exploitation
Optional learning activities

Keep any reflections private. Nothing is submitted. You may skip any activity. These are educational drafts; original qualifications and safety notes apply. Avoid saving personal details on a device someone else can access.

Activity 1: Recognize the power imbalance In pairs or individually, identify who controls a resource in Scenario A, what consequence is being threatened, and why an apparent “yes” may not represent freely given consent. Suggested response: The supervisor controls evaluation; the student risks an educational penalty; this undermines free choice.

Activity 2: Practice supportive language Rewrite “Why did you go there?” as a survivor-centred question. Suggested response: “What would help you feel safer right now?” Do not ask the learner to reveal personal experiences.

Activity 3: Choose a safer digital response For Scenario C, describe two considerations before changing device settings or messaging the threatening person. Suggested response: Evaluate whether the person monitors the device, and identify a safer device or professional support contact.

Activity 4: Sort urgent and non-urgent needs Classify the following: severe injury; a need for HIV exposure assessment after yesterday’s assault; a request for information about school policy. Suggested response: Injury and recent exposure warrant urgent healthcare; the policy question needs a safe institutional information channel.

Activity 5: Map a referral without assumptions Using Rwanda’s publicly listed contacts, outline a referral that offers choice and explains possible information-sharing. Suggested response: Ask whether the person is safe and wants medical/support options, share the Isange contact 3029, explain that providers can clarify reporting obligations, and support safe access without making confidentiality promises.
