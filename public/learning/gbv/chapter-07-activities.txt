Emotional, Psychological and Verbal Abuse
Optional learning activities

Keep any reflections private. Nothing is submitted. You may skip any activity. These are educational drafts; original qualifications and safety notes apply. Avoid saving personal details on a device someone else can access.

Activity A — Recognize the pattern (individual or group; 10 minutes) Read a fictional statement: “If you visit your friends, I will make sure no one trusts you again.” Identify the behavior (threat and isolation pressure), the possible impact (fear and withdrawal), and a safer support response (private, nonjudgmental assistance). Do not ask participants to share personal examples.

Activity B — Respectful or controlling? (10 minutes) Compare: “Can we agree on how to communicate when plans change?” versus “Give me your passwords or I will punish you.” Discuss which response leaves room for choice, and how threats change the situation.

Activity C — Safe listening role-play (15 minutes) One participant reads a fictional disclosure, another practices a supportive response, and an observer checks for listening, validation, choice and privacy. Do not role-play an aggressive perpetrator or reenact violence. Any participant may opt out.

Activity D — Support pathway mapping (15 minutes) Without naming survivors, identify a school counselor, health facility, Isange Centre or other verified local resource and document accessibility, confidentiality limits and the safest way to make contact. Confirm contacts before using them in real referrals.

Activity E — Digital privacy thought experiment (10 minutes) Consider a fictional person whose messages may be monitored. Discuss why “just block the person” or “change every password now” may be unsafe without considering the circumstances. Suggest contacting a specialist from a safe channel when possible.
