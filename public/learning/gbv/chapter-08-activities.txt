Economic Violence and Financial Control
Optional learning activities

Keep any reflections private. Nothing is submitted. You may skip any activity. These are educational drafts; original qualifications and safety notes apply. Avoid saving personal details on a device someone else can access.

Activity 1 - Recognize the behavior Read Case A. Identify the conduct that involves controlling funds, the potential effect on independent decision-making, and one safe question a supporter could ask. Suggested response: Threats and taking earnings are concerning; a safe question could be, "Is it safe for us to discuss what support you would find useful?"

Activity 2 - Healthy or coercive? Compare: (a) two partners agree on a savings plan and both can review transactions; (b) one partner uses threats to demand all earnings. Suggested response: The first is potentially healthy cooperation; the second indicates coercive financial control.

Activity 3 - Identify choices without giving orders In Case C, list options for obtaining independent information without telling the person what to do. Suggested response: Explain that a qualified adviser, legal-aid service, or trusted support person may help review obligations. Safety and freedom to choose come first.

Activity 4 - Digital safety reflection A person reports that someone else reads their mobile-money notifications. What should you avoid suggesting automatically? Suggested response: Do not automatically recommend changing passwords or disabling access, which might be noticed and increase risk; explore safer support with a specialist.

Activity 5 - Supportive communication practice Rewrite "Why didn't you just leave?" into a respectful question. Suggested response: "What would feel most helpful and safe for you right now?"
