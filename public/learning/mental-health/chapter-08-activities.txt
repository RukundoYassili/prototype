Optional learning activities

Guided activity A - My seven-day sleep observation

For seven days, readers may record sleep patterns without trying to force a target. This exercise is for awareness, not diagnosis or clinical sleep restriction. Avoid recording sensitive details on devices that are shared or monitored.

After the week, consider whether patterns change with shift work, phone use, stress, noise, illness, caffeine or caregiving. Bring the notes to a clinician if a sleep problem is ongoing. If recording sleep increases anxiety, stop tracking.

Guided activity B - A gentle evening routine

Choose one or two realistic steps, not a rigid checklist: (1) identify tomorrow's priority tasks earlier in the evening, (2) choose a quiet activity for the final part of the day, (3) adjust brightness or noise where feasible, and (4) allow yourself to rest without checking the clock repeatedly.

Reflection: Which step is feasible in your home? What barrier would require someone else's cooperation? A routine should be adapted to safety, disability, caregiving and work schedules.

Guided activity C - Distinguishing a worry from a plan

Write down an ordinary concern earlier in the evening if doing so feels comfortable. Divide it into "something I can act on tomorrow" and "something I cannot solve tonight." Choose one practical next action for tomorrow. Do not use this exercise to dismiss real danger, pain or urgent needs.

Example: "I may be late for work" can become "I will prepare my bag and check my transport plan." If a concern involves an unsafe environment or a serious medical symptom, practical safety assistance takes priority over relaxation.
