Child and Adolescent Mental Health
Optional learning activities

Keep reflections private. Nothing is submitted. You may skip any activity. Avoid recording personal details on shared or monitored devices. The original educational-draft status and safety qualifications apply.

Five practical learning activities

1. Feelings vocabulary (children) How to practice: Invite the child to choose words or drawings describing a feeling from today. Ask what helped and whether they want to share more. Do not score feelings as right or wrong. Safety note: Optional; no forced disclosure or public sharing.

2. Circle of trusted support (ages 8+) How to practice: On paper, write or draw up to three people or services the learner could approach if worried. Include a backup if the first person is unavailable. Safety note: Do not list a person who may be unsafe; respect a child who cannot name anyone and help identify safe services.

3. Stress-to-action map (adolescents) How to practice: Make two columns: “What is worrying me?” and “One safe, manageable next action.” Choose only one low-risk next step. Safety note: Do not use this exercise to solve situations of abuse through self-reliance.

4. Caregiver listening practice How to practice: One adult rehearses a supportive opening sentence, one open-ended question, and one nonjudgmental reflection. Then switch roles. Safety note: Do not dramatize an actual child’s confidential disclosure.

5. School inclusion checklist How to practice: Educators review one classroom practice: participation options, respectful correction, anti-bullying responses, and referral routes. Identify a realistic improvement. Safety note: Follow school safeguarding policies; record no identifying child details in training materials.
